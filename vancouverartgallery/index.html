Van Art Gallery: AI Installation

Designing an award-winning AI installation for the Vancouver Art Gallery so visitors' movement through the room became the artwork.

Problem

The gallery's head curators asked my team to design, develop and implement an installation for Imitation Game, their exhibit on AI and visual culture, which ran from March to October 2022. It had to gather visitor data without getting in anyone's way, and show it back in a form that was attractive and "accurate-ish".

01Research

Visitors would arrive with completely different relationships to AI, and the piece had to hold all of them at once.

I plotted attitudes to AI on an audience spectrum, from 'the user is in control' through to 'the AI is a companion'. Two personas sat at either end: Steve, a retired builder, and Ashley, a museum learning advocate with a data science background.

02Concept

Each visitor became an anonymised, numbered, coloured path, drawn live on a nearby monitor.

The curators chose my idea: real-time human-detection software plotting movement and dwell time as it happened, so the piece doubled as artwork and research tool. The visitors became the artists.

03System

The line was a vine rather than a graph, so it grew.

I named the piece Creepers, and the name works both ways. It came from the feeling of being surveilled: cameras quietly following you round a room is a little creepy. And the lines climb and spread across the screen like a creeping plant.

I wrote its rules before anything was built. Colour was set by the hour of the day, dwell time rippled out as rings, and lines faded to 66%, then 34%, then out as each session's visitors left.

04Room

The rules had to be explained through the piece itself, so the room was planned around it.

I mapped the visitor journey and exhibition layout from the personas, and wrote the didactic panel. The room held one screen and one line drawing, and was kept dark enough to let the drawing carry it.

05Exhibit

Every visitor was detected, numbered and given a colour. The number and the colour were all anybody became.

Creepers was meant to be understood by any visitor, and to look good enough to enjoy without the deeper meaning. The paths cross, loop back and double up the way people actually move.

What I learned

You can't design one explanation for a room full of people who arrive with completely different relationships to AI. The piece had to explain its own rules.
